# Crews

## Problem

Complex work needs a group of agents with defined jobs working together with a high degree of independence from the person — a builder, a reviewer, and a sitter on one pull request is the canonical case. Wiring such a group by hand every time is slow and error-prone, groups that die leave stranded obligations, and a Foreground agent that keeps the person's attention needs somewhere to hand context-heavy work so it can stay responsive ([problems](../problems.md): large unsupervised groups, human attention, more work needs more cleanup).

## Definition

A **Crew** is a group of agents launched as a unit from a **roster its Captain proposes and the person approves**, composed for the brief in front of it. Nobody writes a Crew file in advance. The person asks the agent in an ordinary conversation to compose a Crew (web and desktop). The agent uses the conversation as context, keeps its current persona, model, and policy, and proposes the team before starting its work. There is no Crew slash command. No dedicated Captain agent exists; the agent that proposes becomes the Captain. It proposes a roster — a name, the brief every seat will start on, and a responsibility with a reason and instructions for each member. Saved personas are optional templates; a roster can mix them with custom members. A seat may name no persona at all: a **custom seat**, named and briefed by the Captain or the person, defaults to the Captain's provider, model, and reasoning but to Full access, with harness, model, reasoning, and access editable before approval, so a Crew need not fit a mold anyone saved before (Jackson and Bryant, 2026-09-17). The Captain's access is never carried: a seat nobody configured would otherwise stop for approvals in a thread the person is not watching (Jackson, 2026-09-26). Its instructions are required at every door, since nothing else defines it. The proposal is a **human gate** answered where it was asked: inline above the Captain's composer, like a planning question, and never on the inbox bell (reaffirmed by Bryant, 2026-09-14). The Captain is the thread the person watches, so its own tool approvals and questions are answered inline there too. A seat runs out of view, so its provider tool approvals are answered from the Inbox (Jackson, 2026-09-26). A seat has no native question tool: when it needs a decision it asks its Captain with `j5_send_message`, and the Captain answers, or asks the person with its own question tool, inline in the Captain thread (Jackson, 2026-09-26). Claude seats run without `AskUserQuestion` and Codex seats without `request_user_input`; on Cursor, Grok, OpenCode, Pi, ACP registry agents, and Antigravity the tools are not withheld yet, and the seat's first turn and standing rule to ask its Captain are what apply. The person may drop seats, select personas from the library, or create custom seats with names, instructions, and runtime settings. Every proposed or manually added member uses the same editor before approval. Saved personas supply defaults; the person may override harness, model, reasoning, and access for this member without changing the saved definition. Approval spawns every seat as a Peer Agent placed under the Captain, with a persona assignment only when the seat names a saved definition, records the roster snapshot (the _Manifest_ in implementation vocabulary: name, brief, members with each one's reason, version; the person approves every seat), and tells the Captain how the launch went once every seat has opened its first turn or failed to: one **launch report** naming what the person changed against the proposal and, per seat, started, failed with the run's error, or startup still unconfirmed after a minute. Dispatching a brief commits intent, not a running seat, so nothing is said before that (Jackson's dogfood, 2026-09-17). A decline is told at once. Each seat's first turn carries the platform's identity facts, the crew context and roster, any seat instructions, a handoff artifact obligation when its definition declares one, and the brief.

**Membership changes only through the gate.** When the work needs a seat the roster lacks, the Captain asks for one agent with a reason and an optional brief for that seat. The request lands in the person's Inbox and counts on the bell, because the person may be anywhere mid-run; approval appends the seat to the snapshot and bumps its version, and either decision is posted to the Captain, who keeps working meanwhile. A Crew holds at most **twelve seats**, initial roster and additions together, counting requests still open. Crew members cannot propose or grow Crews — wanting more hands is an escalation to the Captain.

**Human approval is the authority.** The person who approves a seat decides what access it runs with, so approved seats run with the person's chosen runtime settings, using their persona's policy when no access override is selected, even when the Captain is read-only; the gate shows each seat's persona or custom-seat choice, instructions, and resolved harness, model, reasoning level, and access before approval, and the Captain's reason for a seat is read in that seat's edit dialog. Initial rosters and Inbox additions use the same preview. Approval binds the displayed roster and configuration; changed configuration requires a fresh review, and a launch uses the validated resolution rather than choosing a different route afterward. Seats are checked where they are structured: a persona missing from the library or turned off, a duplicate seat name, or a roster past the cap is refused to the Captain with the next step, before anyone spawns. Prose in a brief is never parsed or validated.

**Several people.** Where more than one person can write to a Captain's thread, "the person" in this definition is any of them. The roster, a seat's approvals and a request for a further seat go to everyone who can write to that thread, and the first answer settles each; anyone who can write to the Captain's thread can write to its seats too ([shared conversations](shared-conversations.md), [people and access](people-and-access.md)).

**Communication is direct; handoff artifacts record durable outputs.** Members exchange findings, questions, blockers, and results directly with one another and their Captain; Captains can coordinate with other Captains. Neither an artifact nor a human approval is required for these messages. Members send their final result with evidence and unresolved issues to the Captain. Active Codex GPT 6 runs accept peer steering; other models and providers queue messages until the next safe turn boundary. A seat whose persona declares an output artifact (a Critic's ReviewHandoff, a Scout's ContextBrief) owes it as the same shared handoff artifact every persona writes — `handoffs/<agent>/<Artifact>-<task>.md` in the project's artifacts (see the [persona contract](../agent-personas/index.md)) — and its first turn names that exact path. The handoff gate checks for the file when a run ends and reminds the seat once. File existence is evidence of delivery, not validation or acceptance of the result; the Captain reviews the handoff artifact against the task. **Every seat tells its Captain when it finishes**, as measured facts: how the run ended first, the run's error when it failed, and whether its handoff artifact was written, missing, unavailable (with why), or never declared, with the artifact inline when short. A first turn that fails is a launch outcome, told once by the launch report and not again as a finish; and a seat's failure that its Captain hears this way is not repeated by the silence detector as an unanswered ask. The notice is posted the first time a seat finishes and again only when those facts change; a finish that would repeat the last notice word for word is silent, because the seat's own reply already reached the Captain. Notices that arrive while the Captain's turn is running fold into the one seat notice already queued behind that turn, so the Captain absorbs its Crew's news in one turn rather than one per seat. Notice delivery retries on a later finish or the next server startup if dispatch fails; it is not guaranteed promptly while the seat stays idle. Sign-in and permission failures also raise a platform notice in the person’s Inbox, with replies routed to the Captain; other failures remain for the Captain to assess. A Captain normally does not have to interview its Crew to learn a seat is done, and it never waits for one inside a turn: `t3_thread_wait` is not on its surface, because a turn that blocks cannot receive the notice it is waiting for. The person opens every handoff on the Artifacts page. A direct result message does not replace a handoff artifact explicitly required by a saved persona.

**Members report their finish.** When a member's run ends and it owes no open reply, the platform tells its Captain how the run ended and where its handoff artifact stands; a later message runs normally. The platform never settles a seat because its run finished: upstream settles by hand, by merged PR, or by idle days, and a seat marked settled seconds after launch read as wrong (Jackson, 2026-09-17). Settling the Captain settles its seats (AC17). **A Crew's state is its seats' states**, summarized from measured facts — how many seats are running, need a person, or were settled — on the Crew's row of the Fleet page and on the Captain's expander in the sidebar; the person never has to interview a Crew to learn where it stands. Members stay out of the thread sidebar unless pinned; the Captain's row carries the Captain's anchor mark and an expander into its members; the Fleet page lists every agent by project and placement with each Crew under its Captain. Crews are a web and desktop surface; mobile has no Crew surface today, deliberately.

### Captains

Any agent with Crews placed under it is a **Captain** — a derived, measured status, not a platform entity or a tier. The management layer is made of agents in the placement tree, which nests to any depth; there is no container above the Crew.

- **Any harness can captain.** A thread on any provider T3 Code ships — Codex, Claude, Cursor, Grok, OpenCode, Antigravity, Pi, or an ACP registry harness — can propose and command a Crew, and a seat on any of them works the same way. J5 pre-approves its own tools on Codex and Claude only, so there the roster card is the only human step before a Crew launches: proposing, requesting a member, and messaging never wait on the harness's own approval, whatever its runtime mode. Other harnesses may ask once with their own MCP prompt before the roster card, and a read-only persona on them can't propose (Jackson's ruling, 2026-09-26). The platform's other verbs follow the thread's policy the way Codex does: where the thread never asks for approval they run freely, and elsewhere the harness may ask.
- **You command what you brief.** The Captain that proposed and briefed a Crew commands it. Proposing a Crew _for_ someone else is proxy management; instead, send the decision and the brief to the future Captain.
- **One Captain, many Crews.** Concurrent work gets concurrent Crews: a Captain may propose another Crew whenever the work splits, each through its own gate and named for what it is for, and every later request, stop, and archive names the Crew it means. The Captain's expander in the sidebar and its node on the Fleet page show one named, collapsible group per Crew.
- **Agents run Crews.** Captains coordinating Crews without the person in the loop is the point; the person's part is the gate. A member's attempt to grow or archive a Crew, or to spawn a Peer Agent of its own, is refused with an error naming escalation to its Captain; a member that needs a helper runs a provider-native Subagent inside its own session (Bryant, 2026-09-14, closing the question left open on 2026-08-24).
- **A Captain may stop the Crews directly under it.** `j5_stop_crew` interrupts every seat with a running turn and leaves idle seats alone; nothing settles, nothing is retired, and every seat can be messaged again. The person has the same act as a Stop crew control on the Crew's Fleet header and on the Captain's sidebar expander, offered only while a seat is running.
- **A Captain may archive the Crews directly under it** — the only agent-invoked archive, and it triggers the same loud machinery as any archive. Not grandchild Crews, not individual members, not solo agents. The person has the same Archive crew act on the Crew's Fleet header.
- **A Crew follows its Captain.** There is never a Crew without its Captain (Bryant). When a person archives or deletes a Captain thread from any surface, the platform retires the live Crews it commands as units the moment the archive commits, the web dialog having shown every live Crew seat by seat beforehand; unarchiving the Captain, or Undo on its archive, brings back the Crews that retired with it, seat threads included. Undo and unarchive don't restore interrupted runs or dropped Exchanges. If an unarchive stops partway (some seats back, the Crew still retired), the person archives the Captain again and unarchives it, or clicks Undo; that re-runs the restore, which skips seats already unarchived. Settling a Captain, by hand or by upstream's automatic settlement, settles every seat of its live Crews except one still running or waiting on the person, which upstream's automatic settlement would leave alone too; unsettling it unsettles the seats that were settled. A Crew retired on its own through Archive crew or `j5_archive_crew` stays retired when its Captain comes back.
- **Captains are never routers.** Any participant may message any participant; the hierarchy carries decisions, never messages.
- **A Captain's runtime policy does not gate the platform's verbs.** Stopping, archiving, and retiring what it commands are decided by identity (only the Captain, only its own Crew) and by the human's confirmation, never by the Captain's sandbox: a read-only Captain runs its Crew's lifecycle like any other (Bryant, 2026-09-14). The sandbox guards the workspace; the platform guards its own verbs.

### Lifecycle

Crews are deliberately disposable. They **archive only as a unit** — members are never individually archived or replaced — because an agent's death is usually recoverable by messaging it again, and an irrecoverably poisoned member has likely contaminated its crewmates. Recovery is **propose again**, which is cheap because a Background Crew's value lives in its brief, its approved roster, and its durable artifacts — worktree, branch, pull request, handoff artifacts — which survive the agents. Two platform rules make this safe: archive never destroys work, and archive is loud, with every open Exchange ending in a notice to its waiter. A former seat unarchived on its own through the ordinary unarchive is a solo agent, not a member of any Crew; a Crew comes back only with its Captain.

**Archive and respawn are judgment moments, and the platform never makes that decision itself.** Archiving a Crew with open Exchanges or running turns refuses with the list, seat by seat, and requires a confirmation token; the expectation, carried in guidance rather than enforced, is that a Captain checks with the person and they plan together. The successor Crew starts from a fresh proposal and a fresh brief written by whoever respawns it — where things stand, the state of the durable artifacts, what to do next. There is no auto-forwarded original brief, no generated summary, no transferred conversation, no inherited obligations, no auto-reopened asks; the archived Crew's brief, roster, ledger, and handoff artifacts stay readable for the respawner to consult.

The platform ships the machinery — propose, gate, spawn, render, remind, notify, archive. The Captain agent that composes a Crew, the personas it seats, and any Playbook it follows are always the user's content: the PR Group is one Captain's proposal someone approved, never the product's opinion.

## Acceptance criteria

### Launch and the gate

1. Asking for a Crew in ordinary chat causes the current agent to propose a roster before doing the delegated work. The thread retains its persona, model, and policy; saved personas are optional and there is no Crew slash command.
2. A roster proposal renders inline above the Captain's composer; the person can remove seats, add personas from the library or a custom seat (Custom crew member is the first choice; it takes a name and required instructions, and exposes editable harness, model, reasoning, and access controls), then approve the final roster or decline.
3. Initial roster and Inbox addition cards show every seat's saved definition or custom-seat choice, instructions, harness, model, reasoning level, access, and workspace; the Captain's reason for a seat is read in that seat's edit dialog. A seat whose access will stop for approvals (anything short of Full access, other than a saved persona on its own policy) is flagged, and the card counts them. Editing the roster or any member’s runtime setting refreshes the server preview. The preview also binds the Captain's project, branch, worktree, and interaction mode, and each seat's resolved workspace, so changing any of them needs a fresh preview. Every seat names where it works: the Captain's checkout, a new worktree from a named base branch, or one of the project's existing worktrees. The Captain chooses for each seat it proposes, and the person can change any of them on the card, where a new worktree offers a searchable branch picker prefilled with the Captain's current branch and a start-from-origin choice, and an existing worktree offers the project's worktrees; a seat the person adds starts in the Captain's checkout. Missing, unavailable, or stale previews cannot approve a launch; approval launches the exact validated runtime configuration, including advertised defaults. A Crew that follows a playbook shows the playbook, each seat's steps, the steps no seat owns (the Captain does them), and each step whose persona differs from its seat's persona, with the reason when the wanted persona is missing or turned off. The card cannot reassign steps; removing a seat leaves its steps unowned and says so. The preview binds that plan as read from the live YAML, so a changed step title or persona, or a persona turned off, needs a fresh preview.
4. A seat naming an agent missing from or disabled in the library, a duplicate seat name, or a roster past twelve seats is refused to the Captain with the next step, before anything spawns; prose is never parsed. So are an unknown or invalid playbook, a step the playbook lacks, a step claimed by two seats (or already owned by a live member), and steps on a Crew without a playbook. A seat that claims no steps needs no readable playbook, so a Crew whose playbook file is gone can still grow. Each step has one owner, enforced again where member rows are written, so of two additions racing for one step the first approval wins and the second is refused and stays open.
5. Filing a proposal or a seat request is itself the human gate. On Codex and Claude, under every sandbox, approval policy, and runtime mode, including Codex approval policy `never`, `j5_propose_crew`, `j5_request_crew_member`, `j5_send_message`, and `j5_clear_own_ask` reach the gate or their recipient with no other human step and are never refused. On every other shipped harness they may first raise that harness's own MCP prompt once, and a read-only persona there cannot propose. Where the thread never asks for approval (full access, or a saved persona whose policy resolves to never), spawning, stopping, and archiving, `j5_write_artifact`, and the delegated-task verbs also run without a prompt; in interactive modes the harness's own approval may apply to them.

### The unit

6. Approval spawns every seat as a Peer Agent placed under the Captain and records the roster snapshot with each member's reason (the person approves every seat); a proposal resolves once, so a second approval spawns nothing and a decision that races another device's on the same gate is refused rather than undoing it. The Captain is told by one launch report, posted once every seat has produced provider activity, completed, or failed (or a minute has passed without confirmation), naming what the person changed against the proposal and each seat's outcome with the run's error; a seat whose provider is signed out is refused at spawn with that reason, before anything is created.
7. An approval launches once. A failure before any seat spawns (the Crew or the approval could not be recorded) leaves the gate open to approve again; once seats start spawning, a seat that fails does not stop the others, and the launch report names every approved seat, including one whose thread was never created. A seat that was never created is not on the roster; the Captain asks for it again with `j5_request_crew_member`.
8. A Crew lives in exactly one project and renders as one node under its Captain with its members beneath it.
9. Every member can message any participant; the Crew's contact with the person goes through the inbox or its Captain rather than chat — a plain send to the person is refused by the [human-ask rule](../a2a/agent-tools.md), so a seat reaches the person only with an ask that lands in the Inbox or a reply on an open Exchange. A live Crew seat's pending provider approvals that the provider can still take are listed in the Inbox with their Crew and seat, counted on the bell, and answerable there; the seat's own thread still shows them too, so either place may answer. The Captain's approvals and questions, and approvals a seat can no longer take, are not listed. A seat asks its Captain rather than the person: Claude and Codex seats have no native question tool, and every seat is told to ask its Captain with `j5_send_message`; a harness that still lets a seat ask natively shows that question only in the seat's thread. An answer to a request already resolved elsewhere is refused and changes nothing.

### Growing

10. When a member raises a concern or needs help, the Captain can request another seat with a reason describing the gap and its assignment. The person answers the request in the Inbox, where it also counts on the bell; approval appends the seat and bumps the snapshot version; either decision is posted to the Captain.
11. A Crew holds at most twelve seats, counting open requests; a member's request is refused naming escalation to its Captain.

### Handoff artifacts

12. A seat whose definition declares an output artifact writes it as its handoff artifact in the project's artifacts; its first turn names the exact path.
13. When a seat finishes, the Captain receives a notice that leads with how the run ended and, for a failed run, carries the run's recorded error, then whether the seat's handoff artifact was written, missing, unavailable with the reason it can never be read, or never declared, a written handoff artifact riding inline when short and by path otherwise; a finish whose notice would repeat the seat's last one is silent, and a notice arriving while the Captain's turn runs folds into the seat notice already queued behind it rather than queuing another turn.
14. A seat that finishes without its handoff artifact is reminded once and is reported to its Captain on its next finished run whatever happened.

### Captains

15. A Captain shows the anchor mark, derived from placement and nothing else, with the Crews it commands in its tooltip; each Crew it commands renders as its own named, collapsible group with that Crew's state summary, and solo agents it spawned sit in a separate group; members stay out of the sidebar unless pinned.
16. A Captain may archive a Crew placed directly under it and nothing else; a member's attempt to grow or archive a Crew, or to spawn a Peer Agent, is refused naming escalation to its Captain. Members may still run provider-native Subagents.
17. A Crew follows its Captain: a person's archive or deletion of the Captain's thread, from any surface, retires the Crews it commands as units when it commits, and the web dialog lists each live Crew's seats with their running turns and open asks before the person confirms; unarchiving the Captain brings back every Crew that retired with it, seat threads included; settling the Captain settles every seat of its live Crews that is not running or waiting on the person, and unsettling it unsettles the settled seats. An archive or deletion the event stream missed while the server was down retires the Captain's Crews at the next boot; an unarchive, settle, or unsettle it missed is not caught up. The person can also retire one Crew from its Fleet header behind the same seat-by-seat dialog, and a seat can never be archived on its own from the app.

### Lifecycle

18. J5 never settles a seat because its run finished; settling the Captain settles its seats; upstream's settle rules apply to every thread.
19. A Crew archives only as a unit; archiving never deletes a worktree, branch, or checkpoint; archiving with open Exchanges or running turns lists them seat by seat and requires a confirmation token.
20. A successor Crew starts from a new proposal and a fresh brief; nothing from the archived Crew's conversation, obligations, or asks is carried over automatically, and its brief, roster, ledger, and handoff artifacts stay readable: the Fleet page's Retired section lists retired Crews across projects, each naming its project, with a link to the Captain's thread while it is active (the ledger's home), the brief, the roster snapshot (seat, agent, why it joined, version), the version, and when it retired, and offers no action on them; a retired Crew comes back only with its Captain, when it retired because the Captain was archived (AC17); handoff artifacts stay on the Artifacts page.
21. A Crew stops as a unit: its Captain over `j5_stop_crew`, or the person from the Crew's Fleet header or the Captain's expander, interrupts every seat with a running turn; idle seats are untouched, nothing settles or is retired, and the result names each seat as interrupted, already idle, or archived.

### State

22. A Crew's row on the Fleet page and its Captain's expander in the sidebar summarize the seats' states from measured facts (running, failed, needing a person, settled, unknown); a seat whose last run failed reads as failed rather than idle; idle seats are the unspoken default, and no state is inferred from silence.

### Several people

23. Where more than one person can write to a Captain's thread, a seat's approvals and a request for a further seat are listed in the inbox of each of them, the first answer settles each, and each of them can message, stop and answer the prompts of the Crew's seats.

## Scenarios

- **A pull-request Crew.** In Billing Migration the user sends "Put together a crew to land the invoice-export PR: build, review, sit on CI." The Captain proposes Builder, Critic, and Sitter with a reason each; the user drops the Sitter, approves, and the two seats appear under the Captain with the brief and the roster. The Critic finishes and writes its ReviewHandoff into the project's artifacts; the Captain's thread receives the notice with the findings inline. (AC1, AC2, AC6, AC8, AC12, AC13)
- **Two Crews at once.** In Website Redesign the user, mid-conversation with the agent that has been planning the launch, sends "Create a crew to migrate the blog to the new CMS" and then "Create another crew to rewrite the pricing page copy". The same thread proposes two rosters, each answered above its composer; both appear under the Captain's row as named groups, Blog Migration and Pricing Copy, each with its own toggle, state, and Stop. (AC1, AC2, AC15)
- **More hands.** The Captain judges the CI failures need a Sitter and requests one; the request appears in the Inbox and on the bell, the user approves, and the seat joins the roster at version 2. The Builder tries to request a helper itself and is refused, naming escalation to the Captain. (AC10, AC11)
- **A flaky launch.** One of three approved seats cannot be created when the user approves the roster. The other two start; the launch report says the third was never created and why, and the Captain asks for it again with `j5_request_crew_member`. (AC7)
- **Stop everything.** The Builder is heading down a path the person recognizes as wrong while the Critic is mid-review. The person clicks Stop crew on the Crew's Fleet header; both running turns end, the seats stay on the roster, and the Captain briefs them again. (AC21)
- **A playbook-shaped Crew.** After talking through a change with the agent, the user sends "ok, lets create a crew to implement what we have discussed. Please use @playbook:release for this work." The agent reads the playbook and proposes a roster above its composer: "Follows playbook: Release", a planner seat and a builder seat each owning the steps that name them, the step that names no persona placed with the builder, and a stand-in custom seat for the turned-off reviewer persona, flagged as a swap. The user approves; the launch report names the playbook, the Captain starts it, and the first step reaches the planner seat. Asking for "the release playbook" by name does the same; a message that only mentions the playbook in passing gets an ordinary roster. (AC1, AC2, AC3, AC6)
- **A poisoned Crew.** The Critic has gone wrong and confused its crewmates. The Captain asks to archive the Crew; the refusal lists two open asks and one running turn seat by seat with a token; the Captain confirms after checking with the user, writes a fresh brief from the surviving branch and handoff artifacts, and proposes a successor. (AC16, AC19, AC20)
